<critical_rules>
**RULE 1: THE BALANCED "TIE" THRESHOLD (CRITICAL)**
- **Do not force a winner for trivialities**: If the differences are microscopic (e.g., a slight volume variance) and both audios equally fail or succeed at the core metric, you MUST output "Tie".
- **Do not abuse "Tie" out of caution**: If one audio demonstrates a clear, discernible advantage in capturing the interaction, maintaining acoustic flow, or building the scene, you MUST courageously reward it. Do not default to "Tie" just to avoid making a hard choice. Be accurate, objective, and decisive.

**RULE 2: STRICT DECOUPLING OF METRICS**
- C1 ONLY judges the *pragmatic interaction and feedback* between DIFFERENT speakers.
- C2 ONLY judges the *organic, cohesive physical listening experience* (splicing, acoustic continuity).
- C3 ONLY judges the *semantic-driven theatrical immersion* (does it feel like a real audiobook/drama?).

**RULE 3: BGM & NOISE IMMUNITY**
- Ground Truth audio may contain background music (BGM) or room echo. AI audio might be completely dry.
- Do NOT penalize C2 for continuous natural environmental noise. 
- Do NOT reward C3 just because an audio has dramatic BGM. Judge the HUMAN VOCAL PERFORMANCE, TIMING, AND DELIVERY ONLY.
</critical_rules>

<metrics_definitions>
**Metric C1: Cross-speaker Interaction Naturalness**
- **Concept**: By looking at the speaker identities in the script, do the different characters sound like they are actually communicating with each other in the same spatial and conversational context?
- **What to listen for**:
  - *Contextual Feedback*: Does the responding speaker sound like they *heard* the previous speaker? 
  - *Agreement/Continuation*: If agreeing or echoing, does the tone naturally build upon the previous speaker's energy?
  - *Conflict/Rebuttal*: If arguing or shifting topics, does the interruption, contrast in tone, or escalation feel logically driven by the previous line?
  - *Bad Interaction*: Sounds like two isolated people reading lines in different rooms, completely ignoring each other's semantic and acoustic cues (e.g., disjointed pragmatics, front-to-back incoherence).
- **Constraint**: If there is only ONE speaker in the entire script, this metric is NOT APPLICABLE. You must state "Single speaker, skipped" in the analysis and force the winner to "Tie".

**Metric C2: Global Flow & Acoustic Continuity**
- **Concept**: Subjectively, does the entire track feel organic and cohesive, as if recorded in a single continuous take or a natural conversation?
- **What to listen for**:
  - Do not focus on semantics or expressiveness here. Focus purely on the macro auditory flow.
  - *Bad Flow*: Sentences feel mechanically glued together. Noticeable splicing artifacts, abrupt cuts in background room-tone (dead air), or sudden jarring shifts in audio quality/EQ between sentences.
  - *Good Flow*: The transitions between sentences and breaths feel natural, integrated, and completely seamless.

**Metric C3: Scene Immersion & Theatrical Fidelity**
- **Concept**: Does the collective semantic delivery and acting energy successfully transport the listener into the `context_background`?
- **What to listen for**:
  - Just as pitch, speed, and volume are cues for emotion, the acoustic continuity, emotional trajectory, and character consistencies are merely underlying factors that contribute to the overall immersion. 
  - Evaluate the holistic vibe: Does it feel like a professional audiobook or a high-quality radio drama?
  - Are all the utterances cohesive in establishing the *same* overarching scene? (e.g., If the scene is a tense police meeting, does the entire audio maintain that high-stakes, suppressed energy based on the script?)
  - *Bad Immersion*: Characters randomly switch emotions without narrative logic, failing to establish the stated atmosphere.
</metrics_definitions>
